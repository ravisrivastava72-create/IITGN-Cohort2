# Can Kalpa's north-star metric survive one slide in front of the people it will judge?

Kalpa Retail is the programme's client: the company whose orders, customers and payments you have
analysed since Week 1. On Monday you used the same template as Uber's to write the metric Kalpa's
growth plan should chase: **delivered orders per active customer per month**, with three guardrails
(revenue per active customer, the discount share of booked revenue, and the return rate) and two
leading inputs (add-to-cart events per active app member, and new sign-ups to Retail-Plus, Kalpa's paid
membership tier). Tonight's lab puts that metric on one slide, presented in three minutes to a TA who
plays one of the three Kalpa heads who lose something under it. Each loses the target they proposed on
Monday: the marketing lead's orders per month, Operations' app sessions, and the head of Retail-Plus's
reorders per member.

**Who needs the answer.** Meera Raghavan, Kalpa Retail's CEO, who wants one number the company
chases instead of three departments' three numbers.

**The questions on the way.**

- What is the slide's claim, in one sentence a store manager would act on?
- Which one number, with its denominator, is the evidence?
- What is the caveat, and what is the action with its cost?
- How will you answer the head who objects?

---

## What goes in each part of the slide?

This comes up at work whenever a metric is proposed to the people whose targets it replaces.

| Part | What to write | Yours |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The claim, as the title | A sentence with a verb: what Kalpa should chase, and why | |
| 2. The evidence | One number with its denominator, or one chart | |
| 3. The caveat | What the evidence cannot show yet, in plain sight | |
| 4. The action | What changes, what it costs in rupees or weeks, who signs | |

**Numbers you may use.** Any number your own Week 1 and Week 2 notebooks computed on Kalpa's orders,
with its denominator, such as delivered orders per active customer in a month. The orders in Kalpa's
warehouse, the database the data platform lead owns, are a teaching extract of 1,000 orders from 340
customers, so their totals describe the extract, never the whole company.

---

## How will you answer the head who objects?

This comes up at work in the meeting itself: the room judges the slide by its owner's answer to the
first objection.

The TA reads one head's objection, which is partly right. Answer it in the four moves and the
scorer ticks each one it hears.

| Move | What you say |
|---|---|
| 1. Restate | Say the objection back until the head agrees with your version |
| 2. Answer | Answer from your evidence, naming the line of the slide |
| 3. Concede | Say the true part out loud |
| 4. What would change my mind | Name the fact that would move you, and how you would get it |

**What the TA scores.** The four parts of the slide, present or absent, and the four moves, heard or
not heard. The number itself is never scored.
